Case study 02 / Autonomous Mobility UX

Safety That Restores Control.

How can safety-oriented advertising address the trust barrier around autonomous taxis?

Research approachBetween-subjects online experiment; N = 131
Study comparisonPrevention vs. promotion message framing
StatusConference manuscript · Status unverified

01 / Research question

How can safety-oriented advertising address the trust barrier around autonomous taxis?

02 / Conceptual framework

Tested serial mediation model
Message framing
Prevention / Promotion
→
Perceived risk
→
Perceived control
→
Brand attitude
+
Behavioral intention
The model proposes that prevention framing reduces perceived risk; reduced risk is associated with greater perceived control, which predicts evaluations and behavioral intention. The data support a serial indirect pathway, not a direct framing-to-control effect.

03 / Research design

A randomized two-condition online experiment recruited participants through Prolific (final N = 131; prevention n = 64, promotion n = 67). Participants viewed mock advertisements for a hypothetical autonomous taxi brand, AutoCabs. The vehicle image and layout were held constant; only the safety-oriented versus innovation-oriented headline changed. Autonomous-vehicle familiarity was included as a covariate.

04 / Key findings

  • Prevention-framed ads produced lower perceived risk than promotion-framed ads (M = 4.48 vs. 5.12; p = .026).
  • Perceived risk and perceived control were negatively correlated (r = −.63, p < .001).
  • The serial indirect pathway to brand attitude and behavioral intention was significant; framing did not directly increase perceived control.

05 / Implications

For unfamiliar autonomous mobility services, safety-oriented communication may work by addressing the risk people perceive before attempting to build a sense of control. These findings concern responses to hypothetical advertisements, not observed ride adoption.
