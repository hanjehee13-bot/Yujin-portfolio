Case study 03 / Streaming Media UX

Streaming Advertising & User Behavior.

How do formative experiences with television advertising shape acceptance and avoidance of ads on streaming platforms?

Research approachExploratory interviews (N = 13) + online survey (N = 487)
Study comparisonAdvertising socialization across TV and streaming eras
StatusConference manuscript · Status unverified

01 / Research question

How do formative experiences with television advertising shape acceptance and avoidance of ads on streaming platforms?

A research-design framework, not a causal mediation claim. The survey compares socialization groups and examines how perceived advertising value relates to avoidance and acceptance.

03 / Research design

Study 1 used 13 exploratory in-depth interviews to understand how viewers react to advertising in streaming services. Study 2 surveyed 487 streaming subscribers, comparing groups based on their formative experiences with television advertising and audience control. Outcomes included cognitive, behavioral, and affective avoidance, along with acceptance of ads as a “necessary evil.”

04 / Key findings

  • Interviewees described ignoring, disliking, or skipping ads, while some reluctantly accepted advertising in exchange for access to content.
  • The streaming-without-advertising socialization group reported the highest cognitive, behavioral, and affective avoidance and the lowest “necessary evil” acceptance.
  • Perceived advertising value was related to avoidance and acceptance beyond advertising socialization.

05 / Implications

Streaming product teams should consider that ad tolerance is shaped by users’ previous media experiences, not only by subscription price. The findings support audience-sensitive ad experiences and clearer value exchanges, rather than treating every viewer as equally tolerant of interruptions.
