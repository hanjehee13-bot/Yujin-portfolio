Case study 01 / Human–AI Interaction

Behind the Virtual Influencer.

Two experiments examine whether human-operated and AI-operated virtual influencers produce different consumer responses.

Research approachTwo between-subjects experiments
Study comparisonMotion-capture vs. AI-operated
StatusConference manuscript · Status unverified

01 / Research question

Two experiments examine whether human-operated and AI-operated virtual influencers produce different consumer responses.

The studies distinguish agency (capacity for intentional action) from experience (capacity for feelings and sensations). Need for authenticity is examined as a moderator of the effect of operation method on mind perception.

03 / Research design

Two between-subjects experiments compared a motion-capture-operated virtual influencer with an AI-operated virtual influencer in a simulated live-commerce product demonstration. Study 1 analyzed 176 participants after attention checks. The same virtual host and content were presented with different explanations of how the host was operated. Study 2 examined whether individual differences in need for authenticity moderated the mechanism.

04 / Key findings

  • Motion-capture operation increased perceived agency and perceived experience relative to AI operation.
  • Perceived agency, rather than perceived experience, carried the significant indirect association with brand attitude and purchase intention.
  • The agency-based indirect effect was stronger among participants with a higher need for authenticity, according to the manuscript’s reported analyses.

05 / Implications

For virtual spokespersons, disclosure about who or what operates the character may shape consumer judgments even when the visible content is the same. A human-driven production process may matter particularly to audiences who place greater weight on authenticity.
