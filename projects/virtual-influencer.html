Case study 01 / Human–AI Interaction

Behind the Virtual Influencer.

Two experiments examine whether human-operated and AI-operated virtual influencers produce different consumer responses.

Research approachTwo between-subjects experiments
Study comparisonMotion-capture vs. AI-operated
StatusResearch manuscript; publication status not specified.

01 / The question

Two experiments examine whether human-operated and AI-operated virtual influencers produce different consumer responses.

02 / Research design

Two between-subjects experiments. The study focuses on perceived agency and experience; need for authenticity.

Motion-capture vs. AI-operated→Perceived agency and experience; need for authenticity

03 / Key findings

Motion-capture operation increased perceived agency, which was associated with more favorable brand attitude and purchase intention. The indirect effect through agency was stronger among participants with greater need for authenticity. Perceived experience did not significantly mediate the outcomes.

04 / Why it matters

These findings suggest that how an artificial spokesperson is operated—and how that operation is communicated—can matter for brand experience.
